## Legislation Struggles to Gain Traction

Proponents of the Protect College Sports Act are escalating their push to advance the stalled legislation as Congress enters its lame-duck session. The bill, which aims to establish a federal framework for college athletics, has faced mounting challenges in securing floor time and sufficient support from lawmakers juggling competing priorities before the session concludes.

## The Stakes for College Athletics

The measure represents a significant effort to create uniform national standards for college sports, particularly regarding athlete compensation and name, image, and likeness (NIL) rights. Supporters argue that federal legislation is necessary to address inconsistencies created by state-level laws and NCAA policy changes. Without coordinated federal action, they contend that college athletics will continue operating under a patchwork of regulations that disadvantage both institutions and athletes.

## Time Running Out in Congress

With limited legislative calendar space remaining in the lame-duck session, advocates for the bill face a critical window to generate momentum. The compressed timeline means that any legislation competing for attention must demonstrate broad bipartisan support and significant stakeholder backing. The Protect College Sports Act's current trajectory suggests proponents may struggle to meet this threshold before the session concludes and new Congress members are sworn in.

## Competing Priorities Challenge Progress

The legislation's stalled status reflects the broader challenge of advancing specialized bills when Congress confronts urgent national concerns. Lawmakers are balancing multiple demands on their time, from budget negotiations to international matters, leaving limited opportunity for college sports regulation to advance. This dynamic has forced Act supporters to become more vocal and strategic in their advocacy efforts.

## Looking Ahead

The outcome of the bill in the current session could determine its trajectory in the next Congress. Failure to advance now may require proponents to restart their efforts with new lawmakers, potentially delaying comprehensive federal college sports reform. As the lame-duck session progresses, the intensity of support from NCAA leadership, university administrators, and athlete advocacy groups will likely prove decisive in determining whether the legislation gains the necessary traction to move forward.
