## Historic Award Recognizes Czerny's Decades of Service

Cardinal Michael Czerny, SJ, has been announced as the inaugural recipient of the Pope Leo XIV Sustainability Medal, a newly established honor from the Catholic Church. The award recognizes distinguished contributions to sustainability and human development on a global scale. Czerny will receive the medal during a formal ceremony on October 13.

## Background on Cardinal Czerny's Career

Cardinal Czerny, a member of the Society of Jesus, recently concluded his tenure as prefect of the Vatican's Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development. During his leadership, the office focused on addressing poverty, inequality, and environmental concerns in alignment with papal priorities. His work has emphasized the Church's commitment to Catholic social teaching and integral human development across multiple continents.

## The Significance of the New Medal

The Pope Leo XIV Sustainability Medal represents the Vatican's formal recognition of efforts to advance environmental stewardship and human welfare. Named after the historical Pope Leo XIV, the medal underscores the Catholic Church's ongoing commitment to sustainability issues. The establishment of this award signals the Church's dedication to honoring individuals who make significant contributions to these critical global challenges.

## Czerny's Impact on Vatican Priorities

Throughout his career, Cardinal Czerny has been instrumental in shaping Vatican responses to humanitarian crises and environmental concerns. His work has addressed issues ranging from poverty reduction to climate change, reflecting the broader papal agenda set forth in encyclicals focused on integral ecology and human dignity. The recognition of his efforts through this medal highlights the importance the Church places on these interconnected global challenges.

## Looking Forward

The presentation ceremony on October 13 will mark a significant moment for the Catholic Church as it formally honors Czerny's contributions while simultaneously launching a new institutional recognition for sustainability work. The medal's establishment suggests the Church's intent to continue identifying and celebrating leaders who champion environmental and social responsibility in the years ahead.
