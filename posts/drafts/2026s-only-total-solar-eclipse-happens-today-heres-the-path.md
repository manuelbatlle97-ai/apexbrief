## 2026's Only Total Solar Eclipse Happens August 12

On Wednesday, August 12, 2026, the only total solar eclipse of the year will cross the Northern Hemisphere, offering viewers a rare astronomical event. The eclipse will carve a path of totality across parts of Greenland, Iceland, and northern Spain, providing observers in these regions with the opportunity to witness the Moon completely blocking the Sun's disk.

## Path of Totality Details

The path of totality will begin over Greenland and continue across Iceland before reaching northern Spain. Observers located directly along this narrow path will experience total darkness during the daytime, a phenomenon known as totality. Those outside the path of totality but within a broader region will experience a partial solar eclipse, with the Moon covering only a portion of the Sun.

## Viewing Times and Locations

The exact timing of the eclipse will vary depending on your location. Residents and visitors in Greenland, Iceland, and northern Spain will have the best opportunity to witness totality, though specific times will depend on precise geographic coordinates within the path. Astronomy enthusiasts planning to view this eclipse should research their exact location to determine the optimal viewing time and duration of totality.

## Preparing for the Eclipse

Astronomy experts emphasize the importance of using proper eye protection when viewing solar eclipses. Standard sunglasses are not adequate protection—viewers must use special-purpose eclipse glasses that meet the ISO 12312-2 international standard. Direct observation of the Sun, even when partially eclipsed, can cause permanent eye damage or blindness.

## Rarity of Total Solar Eclipses

Total solar eclipses are rare events from any given location on Earth, with decades often passing between occurrences in the same area. The fact that 2026 will have only one total solar eclipse makes this August event particularly significant for eclipse chasers and astronomy enthusiasts worldwide. Those unable to travel to the path of totality can still experience a partial eclipse from much wider surrounding regions, though the experience will differ significantly from witnessing totality.
