## Apple's iPhone Duo Faces the Fold Test

CNET is putting Apple's iPhone Duo through one of the most rigorous durability challenges in tech journalism: a fold-a-phone testing machine that cycles through 900 folds per hour. The device has already surpassed the 20,000-fold mark, raising questions about whether it can outlast Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8 in this critical endurance test.

## The Testing Challenge

The fold-a-phone testing machine represents an extreme stress test for foldable technology. By cycling through 900 folds per hour continuously, CNET is simulating years of typical user wear in a compressed timeframe. This approach allows tech reviewers to identify potential failure points in the hinge mechanism—the most critical component of any foldable device.

The iPhone Duo's hinge design will face scrutiny as the test continues. Every fold places mechanical stress on the hinge, screen layers, and internal components. The goal is to determine how long the device can maintain its structural integrity before showing signs of degradation or failure.

## Comparing Foldable Leaders

The comparison with Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8 is particularly relevant, as Samsung has spent years refining foldable technology with multiple Z Fold iterations. The Galaxy Z Fold 8 represents the latest generation of Samsung's foldable flagship, making it a natural benchmark for Apple's entry into the foldable market.

This testing methodology provides practical insights that standard durability ratings cannot capture. While manufacturers conduct their own durability tests, independent verification from tech publications offers consumers an unbiased perspective on long-term reliability.

## What's at Stake

As foldable phones become increasingly mainstream, hinge durability has emerged as a key consumer concern. The results of CNET's testing could significantly influence purchasing decisions among consumers considering either the iPhone Duo or Galaxy Z Fold 8.

The ongoing fold test will provide valuable data about which device's hinge engineering proves more resilient under extreme conditions. This information matters as the foldable market continues to expand and consumers demand greater reliability from these premium, expensive devices.
