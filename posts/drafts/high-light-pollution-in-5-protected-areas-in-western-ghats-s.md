## Study Reveals Light Pollution Crisis in Western Ghats

A comprehensive research study has identified concerning levels of artificial light pollution across five protected areas in the Western Ghats region of Kerala, raising alarms among environmental scientists and conservation experts. The findings highlight a growing threat to the region's delicate ecosystems and nocturnal wildlife that depend on natural darkness for survival and behavioral patterns.

Periyar Tiger Reserve emerged as the most severely affected protected area in the study, bearing the highest concentration of artificial light pollution among the five locations examined. This discovery is particularly troubling given the reserve's critical role in tiger conservation and its designation as a protected habitat for numerous endangered species dependent on natural light cycles.

## Primary Sources of Pollution Identified

Researchers pinpointed worship sites and roadways as the two major contributors to light pollution within these protected areas. Artificial lighting around temples and religious sites, combined with illumination from roads and highways traversing these regions, creates a persistent glow that disrupts the natural nocturnal environment. This artificial illumination interferes with vital biological processes including reproduction, migration, and feeding patterns of numerous species.

## Conservation Implications

The study underscores the urgent need for implementing light management policies in these protected zones. Nocturnal species including insects, birds, amphibians, and mammals rely on darkness for essential life functions. Excessive artificial light can disorient migratory birds, disrupt breeding cycles, and alter predator-prey relationships within these ecosystems.

## Path Forward

Experts are calling for coordinated efforts between environmental agencies, local communities, and religious institutions to address this growing concern. Solutions may include transitioning to warmer-spectrum lighting, implementing directional lighting systems, and establishing light-free zones during critical seasonal periods. The research suggests that sustainable practices balancing human needs with environmental protection are essential for preserving the Western Ghats' biodiversity and maintaining the integrity of these crucial protected areas.

The findings reinforce the importance of considering light pollution as a serious environmental factor in conservation planning and management strategies for sensitive ecosystems.
