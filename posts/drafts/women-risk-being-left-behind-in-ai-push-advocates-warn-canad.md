## Experts Sound Alarm on AI's Impact on Women and Marginalized Groups

Canadian lawmakers gathered Monday morning to hear testimony from several experts addressing concerns that women, girls, and 2SLGBTQ+ individuals could be left behind in the rapid expansion of artificial intelligence. A parliamentary committee studying the implications of AI heard warnings that without deliberate policy interventions, existing gender and social inequalities could be amplified by emerging technologies.

## What Advocates Are Telling Lawmakers

The experts who appeared before the committee emphasized that artificial intelligence development has historically centered on narrow perspectives, potentially encoding existing biases into widely-used systems. Advocates stressed that the technology sector's documented gender imbalance means fewer women are involved in designing the AI systems that increasingly shape society. This lack of diverse input during development stages risks creating tools that fail to address the needs and concerns of women and marginalized communities.

## The Broader AI Equity Challenge

As artificial intelligence becomes more integrated into workplaces, education, healthcare, and government services, the disparities in how these systems affect different populations become increasingly significant. Women and 2SLGBTQ+ individuals could face discrimination through algorithmic decision-making if AI systems are trained on biased data or developed without considering their perspectives. From hiring algorithms to healthcare diagnostics, the stakes are high for ensuring equitable outcomes across demographic groups.

## Calls for Policy Action

The parliamentary committee's examination reflects growing recognition that AI regulation cannot be purely technical—it must address social and equity dimensions. Advocates are pushing lawmakers to establish requirements for inclusive AI development, diverse hiring in tech fields, and accountability measures for systems that affect public services. Canada's approach to AI governance could serve as a model for how democracies can balance innovation with protection against algorithmic discrimination.

The testimony Monday signals that Canadian policymakers are taking seriously the need to examine how emerging technologies intersect with existing social inequalities. As the committee continues its study, the focus remains on ensuring that AI advancement benefits all Canadians rather than widening existing gaps.
