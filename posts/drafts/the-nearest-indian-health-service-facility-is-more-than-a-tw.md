## Federal Recognition Brings Limited Healthcare Benefits

The Lumbee Tribe of North Carolina achieved federal recognition in 1956, making its citizens eligible for services through the Indian Health Service (IHS). However, eligibility alone has not resolved significant healthcare access challenges. For most Lumbee citizens, the nearest IHS facility is located more than two hours away out of state, creating a substantial barrier to receiving care.

## The Distance Dilemma

Geographic isolation represents one of the most pressing issues facing the Lumbee community as they seek to utilize their newly available federal healthcare benefits. While federal recognition opened doors to IHS eligibility, it did not automatically bring healthcare infrastructure to the Lumbee's home in North Carolina. The requirement to travel across state lines for basic medical services places an additional burden on community members, particularly those with limited transportation resources or chronic health conditions requiring regular care.

## Understanding IHS Eligibility

Federal recognition provides Lumbee citizens with access to IHS healthcare programs, including primary care, preventive services, and emergency treatment. However, eligibility does not guarantee local access to these services. The IHS operates facilities based on population density and regional need, and the lack of a dedicated facility in Lumbee territory means citizens must seek care elsewhere. This situation is not unique to the Lumbee tribe, but it underscores the complex relationship between formal recognition and practical healthcare delivery.

## What Eligibility Provides and Lacks

Lumbee citizens can now utilize IHS services, receive coverage for certain medical treatments, and access mental health and dental care programs. Yet the distance required to reach these services effectively limits their accessibility for many community members. Some individuals may face difficulties taking time off work for multi-hour drives to appointments, while others lack reliable transportation altogether.

## Moving Forward

The Lumbee Tribe continues to advocate for expanded healthcare services within their community. Discussions about establishing local clinics or expanding telehealth options represent potential solutions to bridge the gap between eligibility and accessibility. Federal recognition was an important step toward equity, but closing the distance between policy and practice remains an ongoing challenge for the Lumbee people seeking to provide quality healthcare to their citizens.
