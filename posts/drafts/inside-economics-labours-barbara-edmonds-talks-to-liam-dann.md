## Labour's Economic Vision for Small Business

Barbara Edmonds, Labour's spokesperson on economic matters, outlined the party's approach to supporting New Zealand's small business sector while maintaining fiscal responsibility. In an interview with Liam Dann, Edmonds discussed how Labour plans to boost the broader economy while keeping New Zealand Super eligibility age at 65.

## Small Business Support Strategy

The Labour party is positioning itself as an ally to small business operators, recognizing their crucial role in New Zealand's economic foundation. Edmonds emphasized the importance of creating conditions that allow small enterprises to thrive, suggesting targeted policies designed to ease operational pressures and encourage growth across diverse sectors.

## Maintaining Super at 65

A key commitment in Labour's platform is keeping the eligibility age for New Zealand Super at 65. This policy reflects the party's stance on supporting workers as they approach retirement, contrasting with proposals from other parties to raise the retirement age. Edmonds defended this position as part of a broader commitment to supporting working New Zealanders and maintaining intergenerational fairness.

## Balancing Growth with Debt Reduction

Ekmonds stressed that economic growth and debt management are not mutually exclusive goals. While focusing on initiatives to boost business activity and employment, Labour remains committed to responsible fiscal management. The party argues that strategic investment in small business support and economic infrastructure can generate returns that contribute to long-term debt reduction.

## Economic Recovery Focus

The discussion highlighted Labour's broader economic recovery agenda in the context of New Zealand's current fiscal environment. By supporting small businesses—which collectively employ significant portions of the workforce—the party believes it can drive economic expansion that benefits households and government finances alike. This approach attempts to address immediate business concerns while maintaining prudent long-term financial planning.

Ekmonds' comments reflect Labour's attempt to present a balanced economic platform that addresses multiple stakeholder concerns, from small business owners worried about operational costs to workers concerned about retirement security.
