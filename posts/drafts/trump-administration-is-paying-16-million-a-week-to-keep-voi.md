## Hundreds of VOA Staffers Remain on Extended Paid Leave

The Trump administration is spending approximately $1.6 million each week to maintain hundreds of Voice of America employees on paid leave, according to reporting from The Washington Post. The mass sidelining began in March 2025, affecting a significant portion of the agency's workforce and raising concerns about the sustainability and rationale of the extended arrangement.

## Financial Implications of the Leave Program

The weekly cost of $1.6 million translates to substantial taxpayer expenditures as the paid leave program continues indefinitely. This arrangement keeps staff members compensated while unable to perform their regular duties, creating an unusual situation within the federal workforce. The financial burden has drawn scrutiny from observers monitoring government spending practices.

## Questions About Agency Operations

The extended paid leave raises questions about how the Voice of America, the U.S. government's international broadcasting agency, is managing its operations with such a large portion of its staff sidelined. Voice of America is responsible for providing news and information to audiences worldwide, and the removal of hundreds of employees represents a significant operational disruption to the organization's mission.

## Context and Ongoing Developments

The sidelining of VOA staff represents a notable personnel action taken by the Trump administration in its early months. The decision to maintain paid leave rather than pursue other employment alternatives has kept the staffers on the government's payroll while they remain unable to work. The situation reflects broader personnel decisions being made within federal agencies during this period.

The arrangement differs from typical employment actions and has drawn attention from those tracking federal workforce changes and budget allocations. As the paid leave program continues, questions persist about when or whether the affected staff members will return to active duties, and what the long-term implications may be for the agency's operations and budget.
