## Intel Battles Foundry Challenges Amid Sharp Stock Decline

Intel faces a critical juncture as its stock price collapsed 45% in October, marking the company's steepest monthly decline in decades. The dramatic sell-off reflects growing investor concerns about the chipmaker's ability to navigate a deteriorating personal computer market and execute its foundry expansion strategy.

## Foundry Division Remains Elusive Priority

The company's foundry business—a crucial element of CEO Pat Gelsinger's turnaround strategy—continues to present significant operational and financial hurdles. Intel has positioned its manufacturing capabilities as a cornerstone of its long-term recovery, betting that offering fabrication services to external customers will drive growth and restore profitability. However, the division has struggled to gain traction in an increasingly competitive market.

## PC Market Headwinds Compound Pressure

The timing could hardly be worse for Intel. As the foundry business attempts to gain momentum, the broader PC market shows signs of weakness, undermining demand for traditional processors that remain a core business segment. This dual challenge—weak legacy revenues combined with uncertain foundry prospects—has heightened concerns among investors about the company's near-term financial performance.

## Long-Term Strategy Under Scrutiny

Intel's aggressive investment in new manufacturing capacity, including production facilities across multiple geographies, requires substantial capital deployment. Investors increasingly question whether the company can achieve profitability while funding these ambitious expansion plans, particularly given current market uncertainties. The foundry business model depends on securing major customers and demonstrating competitive manufacturing capabilities—objectives that remain unproven at scale.

## Path Forward Remains Uncertain

The company must now convince stakeholders that its foundry strategy represents a viable path to recovery. This requires tangible progress securing customer commitments, demonstrating manufacturing excellence, and stabilizing traditional chip businesses. Intel's October stock performance underscores the urgency of delivering concrete results rather than strategic promises. The coming quarters will be decisive in determining whether the foundry push can justify the confidence Intel's leadership has placed in the strategy or whether further restructuring becomes necessary.
