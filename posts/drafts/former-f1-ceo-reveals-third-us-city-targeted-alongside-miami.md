## F1's American Ambitions: New York Joins Miami and Las Vegas in Expansion Plans

Former Formula 1 CEO Chase Carey has revealed that New York was among the top contenders for hosting a grand prix race as Liberty Media pursued an aggressive expansion strategy in the United States. The disclosure indicates that F1 leadership envisioned a comprehensive network of American venues beyond the two races that ultimately materialized in Miami and Las Vegas.

Following Liberty Media's acquisition of Formula 1, the organization prioritized growing the sport's presence in the lucrative American market. The strategic push resulted in the establishment of the Miami Grand Prix in 2022 and the Las Vegas Grand Prix in 2023, both of which have become flagship events on the F1 calendar. However, Carey's comments suggest that planners had explored adding a third major American racing venue to solidify F1's commercial dominance across the country.

## Why New York Was Considered

New York's status as a major global financial and media hub, combined with its massive population base, made it an attractive prospect for F1 officials. The city's influence over international markets and its appeal to affluent demographics aligned with Liberty Media's vision of positioning Formula 1 as a premier entertainment destination for elite audiences. The potential for significant broadcast viewership and sponsorship opportunities further elevated New York's appeal during the planning phases.

## Current US Grand Prix Landscape

Today, Formula 1 maintains a strong American presence with the Miami Grand Prix in South Florida and the Las Vegas Grand Prix, which has become one of the most prestigious events on the circuit. Additionally, the Austin Grand Prix in Texas has served as F1's traditional American venue since 2012, providing the sport with three established races across the country.

Carey's revelation offers insight into the ambitious scope of Liberty Media's expansion blueprint and the level of consideration given to major American cities. While New York ultimately did not secure a grand prix race, the former CEO's comments underscore the strategic calculus involved in F1's North American growth strategy and the competitive nature of cities vying to host world-class motorsport events.
