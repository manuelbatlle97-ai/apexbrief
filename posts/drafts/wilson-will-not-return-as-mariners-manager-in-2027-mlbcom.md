## Wilson Exits After Disappointing Season

Seattle Mariners manager Dan Wilson announced Sunday morning that he will not continue in his role beyond the 2026 season, marking the end of his tenure with the struggling franchise. The announcement came ahead of the Mariners' final game of what has proven to be one of the most disappointing campaigns in team history, according to MLB.com sources.

Wilson informed both players and coaching staff of his decision simultaneously, setting the stage for what figures to be a significant offseason for the organization. The timing of the announcement—made before the season's conclusion—provides clarity on the managerial situation as the franchise heads into the offseason with critical decisions ahead.

## Season of Struggles

The 2026 season has been marked by underperformance and unfulfilled expectations for the Mariners, one of baseball's historically competitive franchises in recent years. The disappointing campaign has forced ownership and front office leadership to reevaluate the direction of the team heading into 2027.

## Looking Ahead

The Mariners will now begin their search for a new manager during the upcoming offseason. The decision leaves the organization at a crossroads as it determines how to return to contention in the competitive American League West division.

Wilson's departure represents a significant transition point for a franchise that has made the playoffs in recent seasons but struggled to maintain success consistently. The new managerial search will be crucial as the team attempts to rebuild confidence and establish a winning culture moving forward.

The Mariners will conduct their search during the busy offseason period, which typically involves managerial interviews and potential trades or free agency moves. The organization's ability to attract quality managerial talent and make supporting roster decisions will be essential in determining the franchise's prospects for the coming seasons.
