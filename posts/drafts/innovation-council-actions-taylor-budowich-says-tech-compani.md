## Tech Leaders Need Community Input on AI Development

Taylor Budowich, founder of Innovation Council Action, has called on technology companies to meaningfully engage with local communities regarding artificial intelligence and data-center development. Speaking at a Breitbart News policy event, Budowich emphasized that listening to community concerns is essential for both business sustainability and sound policymaking.

## Addressing Infrastructure Challenges

Budowich highlighted electricity and water consumption as critical issues surrounding AI infrastructure expansion. These concerns have gained prominence as data centers—which power artificial intelligence systems—require substantial resources. Communities hosting or near proposed data centers have raised questions about environmental impact and resource allocation, making transparent dialogue between tech companies and residents increasingly important.

## The Policy-Politics Connection

In his remarks, Budowich argued that poor policy decisions cascade into problematic political outcomes. "Bad policy is informing a lot of the bad politics," he stated, suggesting that when technology companies fail to engage constructively with communities, regulatory responses tend to be reactive rather than thoughtful. This dynamic can create adversarial relationships between the tech industry and policymakers.

## Industry Responsibility

Budowich's message underscores a broader challenge facing the technology sector: balancing rapid innovation with community accountability. As AI continues to expand and data centers multiply, the locations hosting these facilities face legitimate questions about infrastructure strain, environmental sustainability, and equitable resource distribution.

## Looking Forward

The Innovation Council Action founder's remarks suggest that proactive engagement strategies could benefit both technology companies and communities. By addressing concerns about electricity usage, water consumption, and other resource impacts early in development processes, companies may foster better relationships with local stakeholders and help inform more balanced policy discussions. This approach could reduce friction between industry interests and community welfare, creating pathways for more collaborative problem-solving around AI infrastructure development.
