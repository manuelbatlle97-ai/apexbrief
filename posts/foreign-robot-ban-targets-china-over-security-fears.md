## FCC Blocks Foreign Humanoid Robots Over Security Risks

The Federal Communications Commission has expanded its Covered List to include foreign-produced humanoid robots and connected power inverters, effectively blocking new models from receiving U.S. market authorization. The regulatory action reflects growing national security concerns surrounding technology manufactured abroad, particularly in China.

## What's Being Banned?

The FCC's decision targets humanoid robots—autonomous machines designed to perform human-like tasks—and connected power inverters that manage electrical systems remotely. These devices will no longer be able to obtain FCC approval if they are manufactured by foreign entities. Existing models already authorized may continue operating, but manufacturers seeking to introduce new products will face significant regulatory barriers.

## Security Concerns Drive Decision

The move stems from escalating concerns about data security and potential surveillance capabilities embedded in foreign-manufactured robotics and electrical equipment. Authorities worry that such devices could transmit sensitive information to foreign governments or be weaponized to compromise critical infrastructure. The Biden administration has increasingly scrutinized Chinese technology imports, citing risks to national security and economic interests.

## Broader Technology Crackdown

This action represents the latest in a series of measures designed to protect U.S. technological independence. The FCC's Covered List already includes restrictions on telecommunications equipment from Huawei and ZTE, Chinese companies deemed security threats. By expanding the list to robotics and power systems, regulators are attempting to create a comprehensive barrier against potentially compromised foreign technology.

## Industry Impact

The ban significantly impacts companies relying on overseas manufacturing for robotic and electrical systems. U.S.-based manufacturers and those producing domestically may see increased market opportunities, though supply chain disruptions could initially create challenges. The decision may also encourage international manufacturers to establish domestic production facilities to maintain U.S. market access.

## Looking Ahead

The FCC's expansion of its Covered List signals ongoing tension in U.S.-China technology relations. As artificial intelligence and robotics become increasingly integral to industrial and commercial operations, policymakers continue evaluating which foreign technologies pose acceptable risks. This ban suggests the government's cautious approach will likely result in additional restrictions on foreign-manufactured advanced technologies in coming months.
