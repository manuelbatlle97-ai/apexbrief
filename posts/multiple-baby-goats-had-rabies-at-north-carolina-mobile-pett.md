## Rabies Detected at Mobile Petting Zoo

Health officials in North Carolina have issued an urgent alert after discovering that multiple baby goats at a mobile petting zoo tested positive for rabies. The discovery has prompted authorities to reach out to all individuals who may have had contact with the infected animals during the facility's operations.

## Health Officials Urge Immediate Action

Local health departments are strongly encouraging anyone who attended the petting zoo and had direct contact with the goats to contact their local health officials immediately. This includes visitors who may have been bitten, scratched, or exposed to saliva from the infected animals. Early intervention is critical in rabies cases, as post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) can be highly effective when administered promptly following potential exposure.

## Understanding Rabies Risk

Rabies is a serious viral infection that affects the central nervous system and is almost always fatal once symptoms develop. The virus is typically transmitted through bites, scratches, or contact with saliva from infected animals. Because rabies can take weeks or months to show symptoms, individuals who had contact with the infected goats may not yet realize they were exposed to the virus.

## Mobile Petting Zoo Safety Concerns

The incident raises important questions about health and safety protocols at mobile petting facilities. These traveling zoos often visit multiple locations, allowing numerous visitors—including children—to interact directly with animals. The discovery of rabies in the goats underscores the importance of regular health screening for animals used in public interactions.

## What Attendees Should Do

Anyone who attended the petting zoo and had contact with baby goats should contact their local health department or seek medical attention without delay. Healthcare providers can assess exposure risk and determine whether post-exposure prophylaxis is necessary. Even if symptoms have not appeared, individuals should not delay seeking professional guidance.

Health officials continue to investigate the extent of the exposure and are working to identify all individuals who may have been affected by the rabies-positive animals.
